# How the contextual interpretation developed

Once the Rail and Bus typologies had stabilised, the research moved from identifying patterns of use to interpreting the areas around them. That transition raised a new set of choices: how large a Rail catchment should be, how intersecting LSOAs should contribute, whether resident characteristics were enough, and whether classifications of night work, urban form or facilities described different aspects of the same places.

The folders here preserve those choices. `lnwc_radius_development/` follows the move from the earlier 1,200 m specification to the adopted 800 m catchment. `independent_variable_development/` records how the wider indicator panel was assembled and checked. The facility-diversity folder preserves an early direct-catchment implementation: its POI count and nine-Group Shannon measures were later retained, but recalculated through the final LSOA-first 20-variable pipeline. Its historical Rail results and additional variants are therefore method-development evidence, not a separate final analysis. LOAC and Spatial Signatures are additional contextual approaches that were not carried into the final Results structure. `historical_bus_clr_lnwc_imd/` preserves the earlier 3,365-LSOA Bus context result that was superseded by the final refit rather than silently disappearing.

All of these analyses begin after clustering. They describe the surroundings of fixed transport-use types; they do not identify the people travelling, explain individual motives or establish causal effects.
